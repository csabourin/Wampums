Membership Fees and Payment Tracking for Scout Groups

Scout group finances are deceptively complex for a volunteer-run organization. You collect membership fees from families, charge activity-specific amounts, track deposits for camps, reimburse leaders for supply purchases, manage fundraiser revenue and costs, and produce reports for district councils — all with a rotating roster of volunteer treasurers who may or may not have accounting backgrounds. The Wampums finance module handles every part of this cycle in a single bilingual platform, replacing the spreadsheet-and-shoebox-receipts workflow that most groups currently rely on.

See all features

Membership Fees and Payment Plans

Each youth has a fee record: the year's registration and membership fees, the payments received and the balance. Leaders record each payment received — cash, cheque or e-Transfer — and the balance works itself out.

A payment plan spreads the amount over several installments, and parents see their child's balance in their own space. The balances-due report shows at a glance where fee collection stands.

Expense Tracking with Receipt Reading

The traditional scout expense workflow: a leader buys craft supplies, keeps the receipt in their wallet for two weeks, hands it to the treasurer, who types it into a spreadsheet and files a receipt that will be lost by year end.

With Wampums, leaders photograph the receipt right after the purchase. Wampums reads the vendor, date and total and pre-fills the expense, so the leader only selects the budget category and saves. Each expense keeps the receipt photo and the name of the person who entered it.

Annual Budgets by Category

The treasurer builds the fiscal year's budget by category and line — supplies, venues, badges, travel, equipment, or any category the group defines — with planned revenue and expenses.

As expenses and revenue are recorded, Wampums compares actual to planned, and the budget screen flags a category that goes over its allocation. The financial report prints for the group committee.

Fundraisers Brought Into Your Finances

Running a fundraiser means tracking who sold what and who handed in money. Wampums keeps a sales sheet per youth for each campaign.

Each fundraiser's revenue shows in the revenue view with no double entry, and a fundraiser can be tied to a budget category.

Revenue Dashboard — Your Group's Financial Position at a Glance

A treasurer should be able to answer "how much money do we have and where did it come from?" without opening several spreadsheets. The revenue view brings together fees, fundraisers and the other revenue you enter, such as donations and grants, by source and fiscal year.

It updates as soon as a transaction is recorded and is written for volunteer treasurers, in plain language rather than accounting jargon. Revenue exports to CSV.

Financial Reports You Can Print or Export

Wampums reports cover revenue by source, budgets, payments and balances due, and print as PDF or export to CSV.

Reports come out in the language of the person producing them: an English-speaking treasurer and a French-speaking chair each get their own.

Questions about scout finance management

Can parents pay fees directly through Wampums?

Not yet: online payment is not turned on. Leaders record each payment received (cash, cheque or e-Transfer), and parents see their balance, installments included, in their own space.

How does AI receipt parsing work for expenses?

Leaders photograph a receipt and Wampums reads the vendor, date and total, then pre-fills the expense form. The leader checks it, selects the budget category and saves.

Can Wampums produce year-end financial reports for our council or district?

Yes. Reports cover revenue by source, budgets, payments and balances due. They export to CSV or print as PDF, in the language of the person producing them.

Is financial data isolated between different scout groups on the platform?

Yes. Wampums is a multi-tenant platform with strict data isolation. Each organization's financial data is completely separate. No leader or parent from one group can access the financial records of another organization.
