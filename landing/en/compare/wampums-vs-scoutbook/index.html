Wampums vs Scoutbook: Purpose-Built for Canada's Bilingual Scout Groups

Scoutbook Plus is the free official advancement tool of Scouting America (formerly the Boy Scouts of America), for its own units. It does many things well for American units. But Canada's scout landscape is fundamentally different — bilingual families, provincial privacy laws, a mix of Scouts Canada, Guides, Cadets, and independent youth organizations, and cultural expectations around French-language services that Scoutbook was simply not designed to meet. This comparison explains the key differences.

See all features

Built for Canada, Not Translated for It

Scoutbook is built for Scouting America’s own units and programs. Its badges follow the American merit badge structure, it is run under US law, and we found no French version of it.

Wampums was built from scratch for the Canadian bilingual context. Every notification, form, and report is available in English or French, and the language is set per user — not per account and not as a global toggle. A French-speaking parent receives a French consent form while their group's English-speaking leader sees the same record in English. This is not a translation layer applied after the fact; it is how the platform fundamentally works. The difference is felt in every interaction.

Badge Programs Beyond BSA Merit Badges

Scoutbook's badge and advancement system was designed around BSA's specific merit badge structure — a list of requirements and counselors that works well for American troops but does not map cleanly onto Scouts Canada's Path program, Girl Guides' badge framework, Cadet training levels, or the advancement systems used by independent Canadian youth organizations.

Wampums supports fully configurable badge programs that match your specific organization. Define your own badge categories, levels, and prerequisites. Whether you follow Scouts Canada's structure, Filles Guides requirements, Air Cadet training progressions, or a completely custom framework for a heritage or cultural youth program, Wampums accommodates it. Leaders build the structure once, and the system tracks every participant's progress against that structure indefinitely.

Canadian Data Privacy — Hosted in Quebec

Scoutbook is run by Scouting America in the United States. For Canadian organizations, it adds work. Quebec's Law 25 does not forbid keeping personal information outside the province, but before an organization sends it elsewhere, it must assess the privacy risks and make sure the information will be adequately protected. Funders and other provinces may set their own requirements, so check with yours.

Wampums keeps each unit's database and files in Quebec, at OVHcloud's Beauharnois data centre. Everything travels over HTTPS, backups are encrypted, each unit sees only its own records, and roles decide who can open a health form. When a family asks, a leader can permanently erase a child's record. Emails go out through Brevo, which receives each recipient's address and the message, and the optional AI-assisted writing receives only the text a leader submits. For groups that handle sensitive information about children — medical records, behavioral notes, emergency contacts, attendance history — this is not an optional consideration.

Offline Camp Mode — No WiFi Required

Scout camps are often out of range. Algonquin Park, provincial wilderness areas and hundreds of remote Canadian sites have no LTE coverage. That is exactly when accurate medical records and attendance matter most.

Without a network, Wampums records attendance, badges and medication doses, and a camp prepared before leaving stays on the phone for at least 10 days. Before departure, camp leaders pre-load all participant data onto their devices. At camp, the full operational record is available without any network connection. Medication dispensing logs are kept locally and timestamped. Attendance is taken normally. When the group returns to connectivity, everything syncs automatically. This was not an afterthought feature — it is a core design requirement that shapes the entire platform architecture.

Medications, Carpool, and Finance Built In

Scoutbook centres on advancement, activities and service hours. Running a real scout group involves considerably more: collecting and tracking medications for camp, coordinating carpool for families driving across town to weekly meetings, managing permission slips that need to be collected from sixty families, and handling the financial reality of dues, equipment purchases, fundraising, and camp fees.

Wampums includes all of these as native modules. Secure medication management tracks what each participant is taking, dosage schedules, and creates a dispensing log with timestamps. Carpool coordination matches families to available vehicles for each activity. Digital permission slips and forms are sent, signed, and tracked in-platform with e-signature support. Full financial management includes budget tracking, expense recording, fee collection, and a fundraising module with per-participant sales tracking. One platform — no third-party tools, no scattered records, no manual reconciliation.

One Platform for All Canadian Youth Organizations

Scoutbook is designed specifically for BSA troops and the BSA membership structure. Canadian organizations are not its audience.

Wampums works for any Canadian youth organization. Scouts Canada, Girl Guides, Air Cadets, Sea Cadets, heritage clubs, cultural youth programs, and independent organizations all get the same full-featured bilingual platform. The configurable badge system supports any advancement framework without requiring BSA membership or conformity to BSA program structure.

Questions about Wampums vs Scoutbook

Is Scoutbook available in French?

We found no French version of Scoutbook as of October 2026. Wampums renders the entire app in each user's preferred language.

Does Wampums support the Scouts Canada badge program?

Wampums supports configurable badge programs that can be set up to match Scouts Canada, Girl Guides, Cadets, or any custom advancement framework. The Canadian Path is not preloaded: we set up your section's badges with you when we open your unit.

Does Wampums store data on Canadian servers?

Yes. Each unit's database and files are hosted in Quebec, at OVHcloud's Beauharnois data centre. Emails go out through Brevo, which receives each recipient's address and the message, and the optional AI-assisted writing receives only the text a leader submits.

Can Wampums work offline at remote camps?

Yes. Without internet, Wampums records attendance, badges and medication doses, and a camp prepared before leaving stays on the phone for at least 10 days. Data syncs automatically when connectivity returns.
