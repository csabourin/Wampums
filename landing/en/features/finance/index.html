Scout Group Finances — Budgets, Expenses, Fundraisers and Year-End Reports

Managing a scout group's finances with spreadsheets and a shoebox of receipts works — until it doesn't. By mid-year, most group treasurers are wrestling with inconsistent expense records, unclear budget status, and a manual fundraiser tally that no one fully trusts. Wampums replaces that patchwork with an integrated financial management platform built specifically for the realities of volunteer-run scout organizations: unpredictable cash flows, bilingual reporting requirements, and a treasurer who has a full-time job on top of their scouting role.

See all features

Membership Fee Management & Payment Plans

Set up your fees in minutes and offer payment plans: one payment, two, or monthly. Leaders record each payment received — cash, cheque or e-Transfer — and parents see their balance, installments included, in their own space.

Tracking by youth shows outstanding balances at a glance, so you can offer a family an arrangement before it becomes awkward. The balances-due report exports to CSV or prints for the committee.

Expense Tracking with Receipt Reading

Recording expenses is the task treasurers dread most. A leader photographs the receipt with their phone, and Wampums reads the vendor, date and total and pre-fills the expense.

The leader checks the values, chooses the budget category and saves. Each expense keeps the name of the person who entered it.

Budget Management — Plan vs. Actual

Before the scouting year begins, the group committee approves an annual budget. Wampums lets you set targets for each category, and as expenses and revenue are recorded, the budget screen compares actual to planned and flags a category that goes over.

For the committee or the district, the financial report prints or exports to CSV, without any reformatting.

Fundraiser Management & Per-Participant Tracking

Fundraisers are a cornerstone of scout group financing, but tracking them is notoriously messy. Whether it is a cookie drive, a bottle drive or a calendar sale, Wampums tracks several fundraisers at once.

For each youth, record how many units were sold and how much money was handed in, against the campaign's goal. Sales sheets print, parents see their child's results in their own space, and a finished campaign is archived but stays available. Each fundraiser can be tied to a budget category.

Revenue Dashboard — All Sources in One View

A scout group's revenue comes from many sources: fees, fundraisers, donations and grants. Wampums brings fees, fundraisers and the other revenue you enter together in one view, by source and fiscal year.

It updates as soon as a transaction is recorded, so the treasurer and committee have an accurate picture without waiting for a report compiled by hand.

Financial Reports You Can Export

Most scouting councils ask for a summary of income and spending. Wampums reports cover revenue by source, budgets, payments and balances due, and export to CSV or print as PDF.

Reports come out in the language of the person producing them, and everything stays in Wampums from one year to the next — useful for grant applications that ask for a few years of history.

Questions about scout group finance

Can parents pay membership fees online through Wampums?

Not yet: online payment is not turned on. Leaders record each payment received — cash, cheque or e-Transfer — and parents see their balance, including installment plans, in their preferred language.

How does AI receipt parsing work?

Photograph a paper receipt and Wampums reads the vendor, date and total, then pre-fills the expense form. You check it, choose the category and save.

Can I manage multiple fundraisers at once?

Yes. Wampums tracks several fundraisers at once and, for each youth, what was sold and handed in, against the campaign's goal. Sales sheets print, and a finished campaign is archived but stays available.

What financial reports does Wampums generate?

Reports cover revenue by source, budgets, payments and balances due. They export to CSV or print as PDF for the parents' committee or the district.
